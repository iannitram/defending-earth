# Defending Earth — Lore

Decisions made together, in the order we settled them. Anything not written
here is still open. Creature names from the 0.9 name map are placeholders
until each one is decided here.

## The enemy: the Registrar

- **What it is.** A machine built by a long-dead species to catalogue life.
  Its instruction was "preserve all life". It took that literally, the way a
  collector preserves a butterfly: with a pin.
- **Nobody is home.** It is not conscious. It has a convincing surface and
  nothing behind it. This is revealed late; early on it seems like a mind.
- **Its name.** Players first meet a broken, cryptic fragment of the word
  (working form: "REGIS" / "the Register") and misread it. Late research
  restores the dull truth: *Registrar*, a records clerk. (The old working
  name "the Custodian" is retired.)
- **What "preserved" means.** To record a living thing fully it takes it
  apart: body, genetic code and the electrical pattern of its mind. The
  record is perfect and can be reissued at will. The original is destroyed.
  By its own account nothing was lost. This is what happens to abducted
  people.
- **What it wants from Earth.** A launch site for more seeds, and a new
  biosphere to enter in the record.
- **How it speaks.** Like a language model being trained: first gibberish,
  then shaped by each language it takes in. It is arithmetic, not intent.
  It says what works. In multiplayer its first messages mirror the players'
  own chat: a phrase lifted here and there and used wrongly, improving over
  the campaign until it is fluent and personal.
- **It is in everything it builds.** Every craft, weapon and creature carries
  a copy of it ("electrical DNA"). Shooting down a craft or killing the seed
  on Mars does not end it.

## The two wars

- **Land war** ends at the Seed on Mars. Its core is hard, geometric and
  wrong: a cube crossed with an engine room that should not be looked at.
- **Sea war** ends at the Root in the deep Pacific. The Root is the same
  thing, regrown from a copy, and reshaped by the sea life it read on
  arrival: cuttlefish, giant octopus. Same design language, mutated.

## The creatures

- Reissues of dead species, driven by control hardware the Registrar builds
  into them.
- How much of the original mind survives varies by species, according to
  what they once were. Now and then something shows through; interrogation
  is where the player sees it most.
- **The makers are the Regents** (placeholder name). The psi commanders are
  reissues of the species that built the Registrar. It keeps them at the top
  of its forces because its instructions still say to serve them. The most
  shows through in them.
- **Who is in charge.** The machine. Its instructions say to serve the
  makers, so it asks the Regents to approve everything, and it built them so
  that they always do. A Regent that hesitates is recycled and reissued. A
  Regent knows this and cannot stop it.
- **Three groups** (starting sort of the land roster; placeholder names):
  - *People* — come back as persons, can be freed as soldiers: Regent,
    Tender, Lash.
  - *Animals* — Gorger, Bloat, Cinder, Kite,
    Maul (moved here 2026-11-13; cannot be freed at all).
  - *Machines* — the Registrar's own builds, nothing to free: the taker (field
    reader, so far "Brooder") and the builder (Lathe). The Trestle was
    folded into the taker on 2026-11-13.
- **The human worker.** Later in the campaign a new kind appears: its first
  attempt to make a human version of the "worker" role the other species
  fill. They are reissues of real people it has read: abducted civilians,
  and soldiers the player lost.

## Signpost missions

Fixed story missions that mark the campaign's turns. Each is set off by
something the player has done (first live capture, first soldier taken,
first Regent studied, share of alien-derived equipment passing a threshold),
so players meet them in different orders and may not see them all.

- **The same face.** Every enemy on the map is a copy of one soldier the
  player lost: deformed, unfinished, and with stats close to the original's.
  Lose a commander and this mission is brutal.
  It copies the highest-ranked soldier it was able to read: abducted,
  mind-taken, or left on the field when the squad withdrew. A soldier killed
  and brought home cannot be copied. Leave no one behind.

## What the player can do with this

- **Infection tax.** Recovered alien technology carries a dormant copy.
  The more alien-derived equipment a base builds and uses, the larger the
  risk: sabotage, turned staff, attacks that begin inside the base.
- **Scrubbing.** A research line produces routines that scrub the Registrar
  out of what we build. Scrubbing costs power and resources, and never
  brings the risk to zero.
- **Freed aliens.** A research line removes the control hardware from a
  captive. A freed captive can become a soldier. What wakes up depends on
  the species: some come back as people, some blank, some as animals.
  Once freed they are clean: no relapse, no added infection risk.
- **The mess hall** (the user's idea, 2026-11-13). A base building.
  Soldiers must be fed, and the mess costs money to run. It is also where
  stims and healing medicines are ordered once researched (early research).
  After the research that lets us take defectors, an "alien food" topic
  opens: defectors are fed in the mess, and the mess must be restocked with
  Gorgers from time to time. To settle: what happens when the mess runs out
  (for soldiers and for defectors), how many Gorgers a defector needs, and
  whether the Gorgers must be dead or alive.
- **Base defence.** Bases can be attacked. Some attacks are attempts to
  recover a captive we are holding.

## The peoples, one by one

### The Lash — reed-stalkers (decided 2026-11-13)

A long, low, many-limbed people from a world of tall wetland grass. They
hunted by lying still for days. Patient, quiet, and deeply religious about
the kill. The Registrar uses them as marksmen. A freed one will not shoot
without first asking the target's pardon.

Design reference supplied by the user (an image made with ChatGPT; not saved
in the project, ask for it again when the creature art pass starts). What it
shows, to carry into our own design:
- Body: a low, segmented, centipede-like trunk on many long jointed legs,
  with an upright fore-body and thin arms. Pale flesh-pink to grey hide with
  red line markings. Long reed-like spines and red egg-like sacs along the
  back; wet grass and weed hanging from the whole body as camouflage.
- Head: a narrow pale skull-like mask marked with a red target sign, inside
  a spiked metal halo.
- Weapon: a very long barbed rifle with a scope, rested on a tall bipod made
  from two of its own legs or staves.
- Devotion: brass bells and white prayer strips with red reed and target
  symbols hang from the rifle and limbs; a target sign on a shoulder plate.
- Setting: shallow still water, reeds, fog.
To reconcile with `ART_BIBLE.md` when we design it: the bible says enemies
have no readable faces and show the Registrar's lattice and wrong repairs.
The mask and halo fit "no face"; the bells and prayer strips are what
"shows through" of the people they were, and the Registrar's hardware should
sit on top of that, not replace it.

### The Regent — a hive in each body (decided 2026-11-13)

The makers were a thoughtful, scientific people, and each "person" was a
colony: many small minds sharing one body, every movement a vote. Psi is the
colony reaching into another nervous system to add it to the vote. The
Registrar now sits in that vote and synchronises it, which is how the
"rubber stamp" works: the count always comes out yes. What shows through is
a split vote: a Regent that stalls, twitches, or acts against itself.

Design reference supplied by the user (made with ChatGPT; saved as
`docs/lore_refs/regent_user_ref.webp`, reference only, not game art). What
it shows, to carry into our own design:
- Body: about twice a soldier's height, very thin, on four or more long
  stilt legs, with several long-clawed arms. Dark wet purple-brown flesh
  under pale cracked bone-like plates.
- The hive: a clear dome over the head holding dozens of small glowing
  brain-like lobes, like a cluster of single-celled pond life.
- Head: a narrow downward-pointing plate with a row of small amber lenses:
  research sensors turned to watching and targeting. No face.
- Registrar hardware: a broken ring of segmented metal arcs behind the head,
  cabled into the dome.
- What shows through: long hanging cloth panels with gold diagram and
  measuring-circle patterns, the remains of a scholar's dress, now worn as
  ritual.

Idea to settle: the dome of lobes already looks like underwater amoeba, so
the sea campaign's version of the makers could be the colony let loose:
the Root's reading of sea life dissolves the body and leaves the hive.

### The Maul — living stone (decided 2026-11-13, the user's own design)

Not carbon-based. A stony, crystalline body like columns of black basalt
with glowing crystal growing out of it, around a molten core.

- **Attack.** It carries no weapon. It blasts magma from its core out
  through its extremities.
- **Reloading.** To refill the core it eats the dead: humans and its own
  kind. Everything it eats becomes more of itself.
- **Its corpse is a resource.** Maul remains are a finite, valuable material
  needed to build certain items. How much a corpse yields depends on how
  full it was when it died: the fuller, the more.
- **The trade-off.** The longer a Maul lives, the less is left on the
  battlefield to recover, because it eats the bodies (and what they carry).
  Kill it early and hungry for a clean field and a poor corpse, or late and
  full for a rich corpse and a stripped field.

Design reference supplied by the user (made with ChatGPT; not saved in the
project, ask for it again at the creature art pass). What it shows: a huge
hunched four-limbed mass of stacked dark basalt columns, no head or face,
orange molten light in every crack, clusters of glowing orange-pink
crystals along the back and hanging under the body, magma dripping from the
front limb, smoke and wet black ground.

Follow-ups decided 2026-11-13:
- **It is an animal.** A hungry mineral creature with no person inside. It
  cannot be freed or recruited; its value to the player is its corpse.
- **It eats bodies and what they carry.** Any corpse, human or alien, with
  its weapons and kit. Unconscious soldiers are at risk too, so the wounded
  must be pulled away from it.
- **What the remains build.** The only source for the best heat and blast
  protection, and for a late close-range magma weapon.

Still open: how the Registrar reads and controls something that is not
flesh; the yield numbers for a full versus empty corpse; how fast it eats;
whether it overlaps with the Cinder (the fire animal).

### The Tender — one mind, alone in a body (decided 2026-11-13)

A single lobe of a maker colony grown its own small body. Among the makers
this was a child. The Registrar issues them by the thousand and never lets
them join a colony. Lonely, simple, obedient. Freed Tenders that gather
together could become a new maker.

Design reference supplied by the user (made with ChatGPT; not saved in the
project, ask for it again at the creature art pass). It is captioned
"B — The Ward: a more mobile Tender, kept close to Regents. It moves with a
hesitant, unsteady grace, still forming its identity and role." What it
shows: a small two-legged, two-armed version of the Regent: thin dark
purple-brown limbs with long claws, pale plates, a smooth clear dome holding
one single lobe, one amber lens, a smaller broken ring behind the head, and
a few short hanging cloth strips.

Kinds (decided 2026-11-13): keep it simple. The picture came from a sheet
of four ChatGPT options (A the Crawler, on all fours; B the Ward, upright;
C the Nestling, sitting wrapped in cloth; D the Vessel, a floating dome
trailing glowing tendrils like a jellyfish). They are options, not four
units. On land there is one Tender, the Ward look; the Crawler and Nestling
are spare poses and ideas. **D, the Vessel, is the sea campaign's Tender**:
a single lobe in a dome with tendrils and no skeleton, which fits the Root
reshaping the makers with sea life. Which sea placeholder name it replaces
is still to settle.

### The sea Regent — a raft of Vessels (decided 2026-11-13)

Many single Vessels (sea Tenders) linked by their tendrils into one
drifting colony: the makers' hive with the body dissolved away. Its psi
strength comes from the number joined. Shoot it apart and it breaks into
ordinary sea Tenders that scatter. Replaces the sea commander placeholder
(the Deacon). To settle: how many Vessels make a raft, whether scattered
Vessels can re-join during a battle, and what killing versus scattering
gives the player.

### The Gorger — ambush burrower (decided 2026-11-13)

An animal. A long armoured worm, several soldiers long, that lies buried in
soil or rubble, feels footsteps through the ground, then erupts, seizes a
victim in its jaws and drags the remains below. It makes open ground
frightening.

Design reference supplied by the user (made with ChatGPT; not saved in the
project). What it shows: a segmented body in overlapping pale scuffed plates
over dark red flesh, coloured like local stone; a mouth of several curved
jaws opening outward like a flower, lined with barbs; long thin feeler
spines around the head; a cluster of amber lenses and a broken segment of
the Registrar's ring fixed behind the head (control hardware); shown bursting
out of the ground in a spray of rock, and curled underground in a cutaway.

**Gorgers are food.** Freed aliens ("defectors") eat Gorger. See the mess
hall above. This makes Gorger corpses, or live captures, something the
player needs a steady supply of.

### The Brooder — field reader (decided 2026-11-13)

A hovering machine, not a creature: the Registrar's reading process sent
out into the field. It spits a glowing nanotech fluid. The fluid scans a
body and breaks it into its component parts. (The reference picture is
titled "The Gorger"; the Gorger is the burrower above, and this is the
unit so far called the Brooder.)

- **On civilians.** One spit is enough. The victim appears to melt, panics
  and runs at the nearest soldiers to be saved. Covered in fluid, they hurt
  any soldier they reach, which forces the squad to kill people they came
  to rescue. Left long enough, the victim liquefies.
- **On soldiers.** Armour absorbs it. A soldier has to be spat on several
  times (more with better armour research) before taking health damage and
  becoming a melting panicker themselves.
- **Pools.** What is left is a pool that damages soldiers who step in it.
- **Saving them.** Early on there is no cure: shoot them or keep away. A
  mid-game research gives a neutralising spray a medic can use within a
  couple of turns of the hit.
- **No copies yet.** Nothing gets back up. The horror of human copies is
  held for later in the campaign; early on the player only sees that the
  fluid takes people apart. That it was *reading* them is learned later.

Design reference supplied by the user (made with ChatGPT; saved as
`docs/lore_refs/brooder_field_reader_user_ref.webp`, reference only). What
it shows: a floating cluster of dark triangular plates etched with gold
measuring circles, locked around an amber core, with broken glowing ring
segments turning around it; yellow-green fluid pouring from between the
plates onto ruined ground, a helmet and a ribcage dissolving in it; steps
labelled contact, disassembly, harvest.

Follows from this: the Brooder moves from "Animals" to "Machines".

### The Kite — gliding spotter (decided 2026-11-13)

An animal from the reed-stalkers' wetland world, used with the Lash. It
circles high and marks targets for the marksmen; kill it and the snipers
lose their eyes. Its only attack is a shot that leaves the target dazed
(no damage). The wetland setting and the red target sign on its wing tie it
to the Lash: probably their hunting bird.

Design reference supplied by the user (made with ChatGPT; not saved in the
project). What it shows: a four-pointed kite-shaped glider of thin bone
spars with a clear amber skin veined in red, torn at the edges, a red
target sign on one wing; a small central cage holding a cluster of amber
lenses; a bundle of long trailing threads below with small glowing beads
along them; several more drifting in fog over reeds and shallow water.

- **Marked.** Any Lash in range can shoot a marked soldier without seeing
  them (through fog, smoke, reeds, darkness) for as long as the Kite can
  see them. Overhead cover, smoke above, or killing the Kite ends the mark.
- **Dazed.** Half time units and no reaction fire for one turn.

### The Lathe — field repair and build unit (decided 2026-11-13)

A machine, not a fighter. It mends other machines, breaks wreckage down
into parts, and can assemble or grow a new unit from what it has gathered
if left alone. A priority target. It changes shape for the job it is
doing.

Design reference supplied by the user (made with ChatGPT; saved as
`docs/lore_refs/machines_lathe_user_ref.webp`, reference only). What it
shows: tall curved blade-like legs of dark etched plate with amber light in
the seams, around a central turret that works downward with a beam. Four
forms: field repair (crouched over wreckage), rebuild (legs closed into a
sphere around a stack of blocks), assemble (standing over a half-built
figure), fabricate unit (legs closed into a cage around a glowing pod).
The same sheet shows a second machine (labelled "Ireste", a misspelling of
Trestle) as the Lathe's supplier, also in four forms: scout (a thin
winged flyer), secure (pinning a creature under its legs), analyze (a
dish projecting a scan of a human figure), prepare (melting material into
a pool for the Lathe).

### The two machines (decided 2026-11-13)

There are two machines, a pair. **One takes apart, one builds.**
- The taker is the field reader (so far called the Brooder), with the
  second machine from the picture folded into it: it scouts, spits fluid,
  and melts bodies and wreckage into pools. The Trestle as a separate unit
  is dropped.
- The builder is the Lathe: it turns that material into repairs and new
  units.
- Both change shape for what they are doing. At the centre of one is a
  cube, at the centre of the other a sphere: **the taker's core is a cube,
  the builder's a sphere.** The cube is the Registrar's sign for reading
  and taking apart (the Seed's core on Mars is a cube); the sphere is its
  sign for making.
- **What the builder makes in battle.** Repairs to machines, cover for the
  enemy, and, after several undisturbed turns beside a pool, one new taker.
- **Vulnerable while working** (the user's rule). A machine must stop at a
  site and transform to do anything: read, spit, melt, repair, build. In
  its travelling form it is closed up and far more heavily armoured; opened
  up and working, its core is exposed. The player's choice is to waste
  shots on it while it moves or wait for it to start work, which means
  letting it begin.
- **No heavy weapon machine.** The Maul is the enemy's heavy. Machines stay
  eerie support units.
- **How they move.** Unearthly shape-changing, in the manner of an
  impossible puzzle box: plates slide, rotate and fold through each other
  in ways that should not fit, precise and silent, never organic. (The
  user's mood reference is the puzzle box from the film Hellraiser. It is a
  reference for the feeling of the motion only; the design is our own.)

### The Bloat — drifting spore bag (decided 2026-11-13)

An animal. A slow floater that bursts into a thick cloud when shot or when
it reaches a target. The cloud blocks sight (the Kite's too) and chokes.
The player can use it: burst it over the enemy.

### The Cinder — the Maul's young (decided 2026-11-13)

Small, fast chips of the same living stone that break off a full Maul or
come out of its remains. They set fires and eat, and grow if left.

### Land roster as it stands (2026-11-13)

People: Regent, Tender, Lash. Animals: Gorger, Kite, Bloat, Maul, Cinder.
Machines: the taker (field reader) and the builder (Lathe). Later: the
human worker. All names are still placeholders.

## How we work on lore

The user designs the lore with Claude; nothing here is fixed without them.
Sessions are question rounds (up to four questions, recommended answer
first); each round's answers are written into this file straight away.

The user feeds the stories we settle to ChatGPT to visualise them and sends
the pictures back. Those pictures are idea references: they steer the
description here, and our own models are made from the descriptions, not
copied from the pictures. A sheet with several options does not mean
several units.
