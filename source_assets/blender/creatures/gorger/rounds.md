# Gorger: judging rounds

Each round: render every picture, open and look at each one, name the
three weakest things, fix them, render again. The buried sign is judged
for faintness in every round.

## Round 1

Weakest three:

1. **The buried sign was not faint.** It had a hard round rim, like a
   lid set in the ground. The dust wisp could not be seen at all.
2. **The jaws and plates looked made, not grown.** The jaws were flat
   blades. The mouth was a flat red disc. The plates were smooth, puffy
   and too clean.
3. **The control hardware read as a face.** The five lenses sat like the
   five on a dice, with the ring arc below them like a mouth. Enemies
   must have no face.

Changed:

- Sign: removed the skirt under the rim. Made the patch reach uneven.
  Raised the wisp density from 2.2 to 10.
- Jaws made thicker. Throat made deeper and darker. Plates given a
  dirtier surface and a rough, hewn edge.
- Lenses given uneven sizes and places. Ring arc moved beside the
  housing and tilted.
- Lower feelers trail flatter along the ground.

Sign faintness: **too strong** (rim), and the wisp was missing.

## Round 2

Weakest three:

1. **The sign still showed a thin perfect circle** on its rim. The wisp
   was now a dark blot, the most visible thing on the ground.
2. **Plates looked faceted**, like a low-detail model. The jaw surface
   had a cobblestone pattern.
3. **The dead form.** The cracked plate was hard to see. The body was
   2.03 m wide, wider than the two metres it should lie in.

Changed:

- Sign: the lift now fades to nothing before the rim, and the rim is
  tucked 2 mm under the ground. Wisp made pale and thinner (density 10
  to 4).
- Plates: smoothing angle raised from 16 to 38 degrees, broader surface
  wobble, a soft noise bump in place of the cell pattern, more soil
  stain.
- Crack in the dead plate made wider, with one side lifted.

Sign faintness: **closer, still too strong** (rim line and dark wisp).

## Round 3

Weakest three:

1. **The wisp was now the loudest part of the sign** in the aerial
   picture. A pale puff drew the eye before the cracks did.
2. **Night arcs were thick white ropes**, not thin sparks.
3. **The dead form was still 2.03 m wide.** The cause was the limp
   feelers and slack jaws splaying sideways, not the body.

Changed:

- Wisp density 4 to 1.7, and its colour moved toward the soil colour.
- Cracks made lighter (shade 0.62 to 0.72 of the soil colour) and
  thinner (11 mm to 9 mm at the start).
- Arc thickness nearly halved.
- Dead feelers lie closer to the body. Dead width is now 1.98 m.

Pictures 06, 08, 10, 11 and 12 and the contact sheet were rendered again
after these changes and looked at.

Sign faintness: **faint.** In the aerial picture there is no rim and no
outline. What can be found is a slightly paler, rougher patch with a few
hair cracks. A player who is looking will find it. A player who is not
will walk past. From 6 m at eye height the heave and cracks are clear.
The wisp is now very hard to see in a still picture. It will need
motion in the game to be noticed.

## Still weak after three rounds

1. Plates are pale and puffy in full sun. They read as stuffed cloth
   more than chipped armour. The chips are small.
2. The dead form looks much like the live one from far away. Only the
   dark lenses, the limp feelers and the crack tell them apart.
3. The surface pose is a shallow S. It fits the 1 by 5 strip, but the
   bend is hard to see from the side.
